The technology

A piston pushed by magnetism, not only by fire

The block, the crank and the cylinder head stay familiar. What changes is the cylinder wall: copper windings are wound into the space around each bore, and the piston itself is magnetic.

1

Energise the coil

The winding generates a field that repels the magnetic piston and drives it down the bore.

2

Fire cylinders in sequence

Parallel cylinders repeat the action in order, turning linear push into continuous rotation.

3

Share one crankshaft

Both the magnetic stroke and the combustion stroke deliver torque to the exact same shaft.

How it works

Two power paths, one crankshaft

The electric stroke

1

Current enters the copper winding surrounding the cylinder.

2

The field repels the magnetic piston and pushes it down the bore.

3

Parallel cylinders repeat the push in synchronized firing order.

4

The crankshaft turns — rotational motion reaches the wheels.

The combustion stroke

1

Intake — the fuel-air charge is drawn into the cylinder head.

2

Compression — the piston rises and squeezes the mixture.

3

Power — the plug fires and drives the piston down with fire.

4

Exhaust — spent gas is cleared cleanly through the exhaust port.

⚡ Because both paths act on the same crankshaft, switching between them needs no second transmission — and zero speed penalties.

Engineering honesty

What stands in the way — and how we answer it

Challenge

Eddy current losses

Remedy

Re-engineer the coil winding pattern with laminated stator tooth geometry to cut induced circulating currents.

Challenge

Heat generation

Remedy

Design a dedicated dual-jacket coolant flow architecture directly around the winding cavity.

Challenge

Loss of magnetism during combustion

Remedy

Line the chamber with a ceramic insulating barrier to shield permanent neodymium magnets from peak flame front heat.

Challenge

Low torque output

Remedy

Lengthen the piston stroke and tune the AI-driven ECU for an aggressive electromagnetic repulsion flux profile.

Every one of these is a solvable mechanical design problem, not a physics wall — and each has a defined line of attack.
